New York

New York CityPASS, reviewed

Five attractions from ten, nine days to use them, $149 plus a $2 processing fee. The pass is simple; the only question is whether your five would cost more at the gate.

What you get

One price, five choices

You do not pick the five when you buy; you decide later in the app.

CityPASS New York is a bundle, not a sightseeing pass with unlimited entry. You choose five attractions from a list of ten and visit each once. The pass is valid for nine days from first use, which is long enough for a normal city break.

The maths

When the pass wins

◆

Five expensive sights

Empire State, Top of the Rock, Edge, the 9/11 Museum and the Statue ferry add up to roughly $180 at the gate — the pass is about $151.

◆

Three museums and a walk

If your list is mostly $28–30 museums, the gate total can be below the pass price. Then buy direct and keep the flexibility.

Run your own five

Practical

What to know before buying

  • 01

    Reservations are separate

    The pass gets you in, but popular decks and museums still use timed entry. Book slots in the app as soon as your dates are fixed.

  • 02

    One visit per attraction

    You cannot return to the same museum twice on one pass, and add-ons such as the 102nd floor or the crown are not included.

  • 03

    Children are priced separately

    Child passes are cheaper, so an adult break-even does not transfer directly to a family with young children.

  • 04

    Compare with Go City first

    If you want three or four paid sights rather than five, the Explorer pass can be the cheaper shape. Our comparison covers both.

Questions

CityPASS questions

Is New York CityPASS worth it?

For five expensive sights, usually yes. For a museum-only list, no — check the gate prices of your actual five first.

Can I choose the attractions later?

Yes. You buy the pass first and choose the five in the app when you are ready to visit.

Does it include the Statue of Liberty crown?

No. The pass covers the ferry to Liberty Island and Ellis Island; crown access is a separate National Park Service reservation.
