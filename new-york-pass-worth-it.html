New York

Is a New York pass worth it?

The honest answer is that it depends on five numbers: the gate prices of the attractions you would actually visit. Here are three trips that go different ways.

3 scenariosGate prices checkedNo sales pitch
Scenario 1

Five big sights: the pass wins

Five expensive attractions at the gate

Empire State Building$46

The pass saves about $30 per adult before any add-ons. This is the trip CityPASS is designed for.

Scenario 2

Three museums: buy direct

A museum-heavy short trip
AttractionAdult gate
MoMA$30

Guggenheim~$30

The pass forces you to choose five. If you only want three, you would pay $63 more for attractions you may not visit. Buy the three tickets directly.

Scenario 3

A family weekend: check child pricing

Child CityPASS prices are lower than adult prices, so the break-even changes with the age mix. Two adults and two children visiting five expensive sights will usually still beat the gate, but a family that plans two museums and a park day should not buy a five-attraction pass just to “use it up”.

◆

The rule

Count the paid sights you would visit without the pass. If the gate total is higher than the pass price, the pass wins. If it is lower, it does not — and no marketing page should tell you otherwise.

Questions

Worth-it questions

Is a New York pass worth it for three days?

Usually yes for a first trip with five big sights; no for a repeat visit focused on food and neighbourhoods.

What if I only have two days?

Five attractions in two days is rushed. Either buy a smaller pass or pay at the gate for two or three sights.

Does the pass include transport?

No. CityPASS and Go City cover attractions, not the subway or airport transfers.
