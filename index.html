How it works

One number decides it: the break-even

A pass is worth it only if the attractions you would actually visit cost more at the gate than the pass does.

01 — Gate prices

Real prices, not brochure prices

Every attraction price comes from the operator's own booking page, on a stated date. Dynamic pricing is noted, not hidden.

Read the method
02 — Break-even

The number that matters

Divide the pass price by the number of attractions it covers. That average is what each visit must be worth.

Process

From wishlist to verdict

The same three steps work for every city on the site.

1

List the paid sights

Write down what you would actually visit, not what a pass includes.

2

Add the gate prices

Our calculator uses checked prices where we have them and lets you enter the rest.

3

Buy the pass — or skip it

If the pass costs more than your list, we say so. That is the whole point.

Questions

Pass questions

Is a tourist pass worth it?

Only if the attractions you would genuinely visit cost more at the gate than the pass. The calculator does that maths with checked prices.

Which cities are checked?

New York has verified gate prices and pass maths. Philadelphia, Boston and Chicago have verified CityPASS prices and inclusions; their individual gate prices are still being checked and are labelled as such.

Do you earn commission?

Not at launch. If that changes, every affected page will say so before the link.
